01 / Source

Start with a source image, grounded in reality.

We start with a source frame, usually a real-world image, like a headshot or a portrait, which gives the visual effect transformation an anchor.

02 / Transformation

Push the frame into a stylized visual direction.

Composition, lighting, costume, and atmosphere become the design brief for the next pass. Styles can vary from cinematic to artistic, and the AI can be guided to explore multiple directions.

03 / Motion

Bring the frame to life with motion and continuity.

Alien! takes a polished headshot and seals it inside a sweaty, industrial sci-fi nightmare. The lab turns an ordinary portrait into a Nostromo-style survival beat, complete with shadowy corridors, a looming xenomorph, storyboarded suspense, and a cinematic reveal built around pure close-quarters dread.

DnD VFX Stage

A tabletop hero becomes a cinematic production stage.

Krangor turns a personal reference into character art, beat boards, score, key art, and motion while the scroll acts as the production timeline.

01 / Reference

A real person becomes the anchor for a fantasy hero.

The stage starts with a grounded reference so the transformation can keep a human center while the world gets more mythic.

02 / Character

Identity, armor, weapon, and palette lock into a repeatable design.

Krangor becomes a production character: readable silhouette, ornate holy-avenger weapon, warm metal, white cloth, and ember-lit atmosphere.

03 / Storyboard

The shot list turns the character into a scene.

Beat boards sketch the action rhythm, camera language, lighting notes, and emotional escalation before the sequence moves.

04 / Score

The cinematic audio score gives the reveal its pulse.

The score is treated as part of the VFX stage, not an afterthought: mood, scale, and timing become another production layer.

05 / Keyframe

The final frame sells the scale of the world.

A poster-like keyframe packages the character, threat, environment, and graphic tone into a single production target.

06 / Motion

The stage resolves into a motion test.

The moving plate stays lightweight on the page until requested by the viewport, preserving the cinematic reveal without preloading the full file.

Generative Film / Storyboarding

A concept unfolds into generated film language.

The Rock is used here as an existing long-form experiment: book concept, world, characters, trailer, and episodic motion.

01 / Story

A source idea becomes a production spine.

The manuscript opens with Mara Vance's survey team at Uluru uncovering an impossible void: smooth planes, machined seams, metallic dust, a failed probe contact, and the first hint that the rock may actually be a vessel.

02 / World

The environment starts carrying the plot.

Locations, atmosphere, and visual continuity turn the concept into a place the camera can revisit.

03 / Character

Character design gives the world a point of view.

Recurring figures, wardrobe, and visual roles make a generated sequence feel less disposable.

04 / Sequence

The trailer turns the system into motion.

The trailer condenses the Uluru anomaly into a tense teaser arc, moving from field survey realism to first-contact dread before the full episodic plan is revealed.
